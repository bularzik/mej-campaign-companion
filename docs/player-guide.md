# Campaign Companion — Player Guide

If your GM runs their game with Monk's Enhanced Journal and a module called Campaign Companion, this guide covers everything you'll actually see and do with it: reading and writing session recaps, finding people and places your GM has written up, and reading a relationship graph and secrets as your character learns things in the story. None of it changes how you play — it changes what you can look up and contribute to between sessions.

If you're running the game rather than playing in it, read the [GM guide](gm-guide.md) instead — this one is written from a player's seat.

## What your GM's Campaign Companion means for you

The piece you'll interact with most directly is the **Session** page — one per game session, opened the same way as any other journal entry your GM shares with you. Here's what one looks like:

![Session 12 — Shadows over Daggerford, showing the GM's recap and the Player Recaps section below it](images/session-sheet-player.png)

At the top is your GM's own recap of what happened. Below it, under **Player Recaps**, is space for you and the rest of the table to add your own — what your character remembers, noticed, or wants to flag for next time.

## Reading session pages & writing your recap

Your GM will let you know how to find a session page — usually by sharing it directly in Foundry, or by pointing you to it in the campaign's searchable index (more on that below). Once it's open, the recap at the top is your GM's to write; you can read it any time, but you can't edit it.

Your own recap is the block under **Player Recaps**. Click the pencil icon next to it to open the editor, type your recap, and save:

![The recap editor open mid-edit, showing typed text with formatting controls above it](images/recap-editing.png)

Click the pencil again to close the editor once you're done — that's the reliable way to close out. If you navigate away instead, Foundry may still pop up a "You have unsaved changes" dialog; don't let that worry you. Your recap saves as soon as the field loses focus (clicking elsewhere or navigating away both count), so by the time that dialog can appear, your text is already saved — it's just Foundry's editor being unable to tell that a saved field isn't dirty anymore. It's safe to answer "discard" if you see it.

## Finding things

Once your campaign has more than a few sessions, you won't always remember which entry mentioned what. Your GM's **Campaign Hub** is the home base for the whole campaign, and part of it is available to you too — an index of every person, place, and other entry your GM has shared with you, and a search box that's faster than scrolling.

Type a query into the Hub's Search pane and results appear as you type, matching across names, descriptions, and other campaign text:

![The Hub's Search pane, showing results for the query "caravan" across two Persons, a Session, and a Quest](images/hub-search.png)

(The screenshot above happens to have been taken from the GM's own seat, so it includes a line of GM-only detail under "Session 12" that a player would never see. Your own search results only ever turn up what your permissions allow — the same rule that governs everything else in this guide.)

Every entry also has a "Mentioned in" panel listing which other entries link to it — handy for tracing how two people or places you've heard of connect to each other. And if your GM has set up any saved views in the Hub's Dashboards, you may see those too — they work like a ready-made search, filtered the same way as everything else.

## The relationship graph

The Hub also has a relationship graph — a visual map of how people, places, and other entries connect, built from the relationships your GM has drawn between them. Open it from the graph icon and you'll see the whole campaign at once, connected by lines wherever there's a relationship you're allowed to see:

![The relationship graph in whole-campaign mode, showing Captain Aldric Vane connected to The Missing Caravan, Mira Thornwood connected to The Gilded Flagon, and separate unconnected nodes for Serena of the Vale, Session 12 — Shadows over Daggerford, and Old Toby Rackett](images/graph-player.png)

Don't be surprised if your graph looks sparser than what your GM sees on their own screen — you'll see every entry that's relevant to you, but a line only appears between two of them once that specific relationship has been made visible to you. A node sitting there with no lines just means the graph knows about it, but not (yet) how it connects to anything else, from where you're standing.

## When secrets are revealed to you

Some entries in your GM's campaign hold secrets — things your character doesn't know yet, hidden from you until the right moment in the story. You won't see any sign of them until your GM decides to reveal one to you specifically.

When that happens, you'll get a private whisper in your chat log, naming which entry the secret came from and showing you its text right there:

![A private chat whisper from the Gamemaster: "A secret from The Missing Caravan has been revealed to you," followed by the secret's text and a link to the entry](images/reveal-whisper.png)

From then on, that secret is also visible on the entry's own page, marked with a highlight showing it's something that's been revealed to you:

![The Missing Caravan's description page, with the revealed secret "The caravan was never attacked — Aldric staged it." shown highlighted with a left border](images/revealed-secret-player.png)

If you're curious how your GM manages reveals behind the scenes, that's covered in the [GM guide's secrets section](gm-guide.md#secrets) — worth asking your GM about directly, since it's their call how and when things get revealed.

## The campaign timeline

The Hub's **Timeline** pane lays out your campaign's key moments in order — a running record your GM builds up as the story goes, sometimes tied to an in-world date, sometimes not:

![The Hub's Timeline pane, listing four timepoints: "The Caravan Departs" (with The Missing Caravan attached), "Mira's Warning", "The Ambush (staged)", and "Session 12 Convenes"](images/hub-timeline.png)

(This shot is also from the GM's own seat — the pencil and trash icons on each row and the "+ Add Timepoint" button along the bottom are editing controls only your GM can use. What you'll see is the same timeline, read-only: the labels, the order, and anything attached to each point.)

Three buttons at the top change how the list is ordered: by the order your GM arranged them in, by when each one was added, or — for the ones your GM gave an in-world date — by that campaign date. A timepoint can also carry a document or image attached to it, like "The Caravan Departs" above, which links straight through to The Missing Caravan.

## Quick answers

**Why can't I see X?** Almost everything in Campaign Companion — links, search results, graph edges — filters to what your permissions allow. If something's missing, it's not a bug; it just hasn't been shared with you (yet).

**Why didn't my name get linked?** When your GM types a name and it turns into a clickable link automatically, that only happens when every reader of that page would also be allowed to see what it links to. If a link would point somewhere you or another reader of that page can't see, it's left as plain text instead.

**Can the GM read my recap?** Yes. Recaps are visible to the whole table, GM included — the Player Recaps section isn't private between you and your character, it's part of the shared session record.
